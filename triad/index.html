TRIAD

Triadic Relational Interests, Attractions, and Dynamics

A multilevel framework for describing consensual threesome-related interest, participant configuration, attraction, roles, power, consent, relationship context, and outcomes—without reducing the subject to a yes-or-no preference.

The five TRIAD domains

Interested in what, with whom, in which role, and under what conditions?

The framework separates configuration, relational position, attraction, intention, and context so a single statement of “interest” does not conceal fundamentally different experiences.

T

Triadic Topology and Configuration

Who is present, how participants are related, how direct interaction is organized, and whether the configuration is event-limited or continuing.

Three nested levels

A triad is more than three people in one room.

TRIAD analyzes the individual, every dyadic channel, and the emergent group-level experience.

01

Individual participants

Each person brings distinct identities, attractions, motives, roles, boundaries, emotional responses, and degrees of agency.

  • Personal desire and intention
  • Consent to specific interactions
  • Preferred role and meaning
02

Pairwise relationships

A three-person system contains A–B, A–C, and B–C relationships, each with its own trust, attraction, power, history, and desired future.

  • Three relational channels
  • Six directed attractions
  • Different boundaries per dyad
03

Emergent triadic system

Group chemistry, pacing, inclusion, coordination, shared fantasy, and multiplex consent cannot be inferred from the dyads alone.

  • Attention balance
  • Collective pacing
  • “Three-personness”

Original testable constructs

Concepts designed for sharper description and future validation.

↗

Attraction topology

Who is attracted to whom, in what direction, and whether attraction concerns individuals, pair chemistry, or the group as a whole.

◎

Contact topology

Which interactions are desired, allowed, avoided, observed, or contingent for each pair and the group.

⇄

Role congruence

The degree to which participants’ desired attention, initiation, erotic, relational, and power roles can coexist.

Δ

Fantasy–enactment distance

The gap between enjoying a fantasy and wanting, intending, or feeling able to enact it.

⌁

Couple-protection load

How heavily rules and decision structures prioritize a preexisting dyad over the autonomy and experience of another participant.

✓

Multiplex consent

Consent treated as specific across participants, acts, observation, attention, information, recording, and ongoing relational implications.

Ethical boundaries

TRIAD describes interest. It does not authorize behavior.

It must not determine a “true” sexual orientation. A participant's presence, anatomy, gender, or role does not by itself establish attraction or identity.
It must not certify readiness or predict consent. Readiness is contextual, and consent remains active, specific, revocable, and person-to-person.
It must not infer that fantasy should be enacted. A meaningful fantasy can remain entirely fantasy.
It must not rank configurations morally. Reciprocal, hinge, focused, observer-integrated, event-limited, and continuing structures are descriptive categories.

Place TRIAD in the wider framework suite.

MOSAIC describes the person-level profile; SCOPE describes the larger relationship ecology in which a triadic event or relationship may occur.
